KEY FINDINGS:
1. Default is U-SHAPED in age (trough ~0.174 at 50-55; rises to 0.294 at 70+). A linear corr (-0.044)
   reports only the NET TILT and ERASES the curvature. Tony flagged this before the run: "age could predict
   default either direction; we might need band-level analysis." Confirmed — both tails default high, for
   different reasons (young=thin file/income instability; old=mortality/income shock/retirement).
2. PRICING (int_rate) is MONOTONE DECREASING in age across the ENTIRE range (14.5% -> 11.9%), INCLUDING the
   up-slope where realized default RISES. At 70+: priced 12.3% but defaults 29.4%; at 18-25: priced 14.5%
   but defaults LESS (22.9%). The discretionary lever (rate) and the lawful target (default) DIVERGE in sign
   for the entire 55+ population.
3. FORENSIC IMPLICATION: an auditor checking "does pricing track default in the right direction" GLOBALLY
   sees a sensible monotone gradient and MISSES that it is wrong-signed for 55+. The model carries an age
   DIRECTION the default data does not support at the tails. This is a NON-MONOTONE-target / monotone-lever
   gap — a forensic signature distinct from simple disparate impact, and invisible to a linear/global audit.
4. CAVEAT: est_age is a credit-tenure floor, not true age; old tail partially censored (a 70yo and 45yo with
   25+yr history collapse), so the up-slope is UNDERSTATED if anything. Sign of divergence is robust; exact
   magnitude at the old tail is a floor estimate. Pricing reflects LC's grade model, not a counterfactual.
