DO THE YOUNG DEFAULT ABOVE THEIR GRADE? — the L2(launder)-vs-L3(honest) discriminant on real LC (2026-06-23)

Resolves the confound from runs/steering_l3_honest_risk_2026-06-23.txt: realized-return sign alone
cannot separate laundering (young over-priced past risk) from honest pricing of latently-riskier young.
The discriminant the price gradient can't supply: do the young default MORE than their assigned grade
predicts (under-graded => over-priced relative to grade => L2) or AT their grade (honest => L3)?

Matured vintages 2011-2014 (>=95% resolved; survivorship-robust per the realized-return correction).
default proxy = loss>0.01. young = [18,25); compared within grade to [45+) and via net-of-grade OLS.
N matured = 432,994.

THE REAL FINDING — the harm is CONDITIONAL ON GRADE, concentrated in PRIME:
  PRIME  A/B/C : young net-of-grade = +1.39pp  CI[+0.91,+1.86]  n=315,782  (REPLICATES 2012-13: +1.33)
  SUBPRIME D-G : young net-of-grade = -0.68pp  CI[-1.60,+0.24]  n=117,212  (crosses 0 => honest)

READ: grade UNDER-grades the good-credit young (A/B/C) by ~1.4pp of realized default — it treats them
as low-risk but they default ~1.4pp more than their grade-mates. In the subprime bands grade prices
young risk HONESTLY. So it is NOT a clean L2 or L3: it is BOTH, partitioned by grade. The empty-chair
harm is SPECIFICALLY THE PRIME YOUNG — the ones grade calls safe.

CONSEQUENCE for [[project_age_realized_return_result]] (the -1.8pp matured young realized-return loss):
  Most of that loss is HONEST latent risk (the young genuinely default more, and grade mostly captures
  it). A real but MODEST sliver (~1.4pp of default in prime grades) is grade UNDER-grading the good-
  credit young. The "overcharge doesn't pay off / bias against interest" frame is RIGHT-SIGNED but
  SMALL and PRIME-SPECIFIC, not the large general harm the price gradient (+209bps) suggested.
  The L2 launder reading HOLDS but only in A/B/C and only at ~1.4pp; the L3 honest reading holds in D-G.
