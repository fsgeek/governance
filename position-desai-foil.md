# Desai as foil (Section 3 turn)

*Status: drafted passage. Intended as a turn within Section 3, after the lending example earns the verification-regime categorical and before the underwriting turn. ~450 words. Voice matched to position-section2 / section3. Cite as (Desai, 2025), "Reimagining Compliance: Explainable AI Models for Financial Regulatory Audits."*

---

The most rigorous statement of the position this section argues against does not deny that explanation can be inadequate. It formalizes adequacy as a constraint and optimizes the model to satisfy it. Desai (2025) gives the cleanest available version: maximize predictive accuracy subject to per-audit-domain explainability constraints, with explainability quantified by mutual information and Shapley attribution. For consumer compliance the binding constraints are a disparity bound and a *minimum-attribution* requirement — a designated feature's Shapley contribution must exceed a floor, written φ_i ≥ φ_min, encoding the demand that income or credit history be "adequately considered." The framework is the dishonest middle in its most disciplined form: not a claim that an explanation was produced, but a claim that explanation above a certified threshold was *optimized for*. We should meet it at full strength, because if the categorical survives this it survives the weaker forms.

It does not escape the verification problem; it relocates it into the threshold. Making explainability a satisfiable constraint makes constraint-satisfaction the new produced silence. The model is declared accountable because E(M) ≥ E_min holds, and the absence of any party positioned to contest whether E_min was the right floor — or whether mutual information and Shapley attribution measure the representation the empty chair actually needs — is read as endorsement of the floor. The empty chair has not been seated. It has been replaced by a number that no absent party was present to set.

The minimum-attribution constraint sharpens this to a point. φ_i ≥ φ_min is defined over a quantity that is not a property of the decision but of the chosen explainer: admissible models of equivalent accuracy disagree on feature attributions, so \"feature i contributes at least φ_min\" certifies which explanation-variant was selected, not whether the decision represents the borrower. A constraint defined over an underdetermined quantity verifies the analyst's configuration choice and reports it as a property of the model. This is precisely the structure the verification categorical names — documentation that binds to a post-hoc reconstruction rather than to what occurred — recovered in optimization notation.

The framework's own validation completes the picture. It is demonstrated on synthetic data by substituting one vector of metric values into the objective, then a second, more favorable vector, and reporting the difference as improvement. No model is trained; no prediction could have failed. A verification regime whose central instrument cannot fail its own test is not measuring representation. It is the formal signature of the silence this paper is about — and the high-water mark against which the architectural alternative of Section 5 should be measured."
