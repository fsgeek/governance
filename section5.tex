\section{Architectural Primitive Categories}
\label{sec:primitives}

The empty-chair frame produces interpretive structure (Section~\ref{sec:frame-as-method}), a taxonomy of evidentiary claims (Section~\ref{sec:verification-regimes}), and diagnostic techniques for design and examination (Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}). This section names four provisional architectural requirements generated by that frame for systems intended to support those diagnostics. The treatment is at the property level: each requirement is described by what it contributes and what it cannot establish. Implementation specification belongs to follow-on work.

A framing note before the requirements are developed. The four are intended to make otherwise unavailable evidence more inspectable, not merely to improve existing metrics. They respond to the inference gaps identified in Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}: temporal capture addresses later reconstruction; evidence binding makes declared relationships traversable; multidimensional characterization preserves insufficiency rather than forcing a single score; and drift typology separates changes that warrant different investigation. None supplies truth by architecture alone.

The requirements are not exhaustive, necessary for every system, or sufficient for sound governance. They are the categories this frame generates for architectures intended to answer its three diagnostic questions. A community bank may need additional or different capabilities depending on its models, decisions, legal duties, and resources.

\subsection{Primitive 1: Tamper-evident temporal capture}
\label{sec:primitives:temporal-capture}

The property: the architecture captures decision context at the moment the decision is made, in a form that binds the captured context to the moment of capture, that prevents retroactive alteration of either the context or the temporal binding, and that can be later inspected without reliance on the institution's reconstruction of what was captured.

The diagnostic technique that motivates this primitive is pages missing from the ledger (Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}, Technique~2). The technique reads patterns of presence and absence in attested decisions as information about institutional posture. That reading requires evidence of when an attestation was committed and whether its content later changed. Without those properties, an institution can produce a later attestation that resembles contemporaneous capture.

The property has three components. First, \emph{contemporaneous capture}: the attestation is produced as the decision is made, not reconstructed afterward. Second, \emph{temporal binding}: the attestation's claim to a particular time of capture is itself attested in a way that resists later alteration. Third, \emph{content binding}: later changes to captured content are detectable.

The empty chair this primitive most directly serves is the institution's future self. An examiner arriving in three years to review decisions made now can rely on contemporaneous attestation in a way they cannot rely on reconstructed narrative; the future institution's exposure to enforcement risk from gradually-degraded documentation is structurally addressed by capture that cannot be retroactively improved.

The primitive also serves the future compliance officer (institutional knowledge survives personnel turnover in a form that does not depend on the departing officer's narrative reconstruction) and indirectly serves the customer affected by an AI decision (the customer's challenge to a decision made years prior can rely on what the institution attested at the time of decision, rather than on what the institution claims now to remember).

Together, these components establish when particular content was committed and whether later change is detectable. They do not establish that the captured content is complete or true. For example, temporal and content binding without contemporaneous capture can authenticate a later reconstruction; the signature establishes commitment to that reconstruction, not its accuracy as a decision-time record.

\subsection{Primitive 2: Evidence binding}
\label{sec:primitives:evidence-binding}

The property: the architecture binds decisions to the evidence that supported them, in a way that the binding can be later inspected, traversed, and verified. A decision attested without evidence is not the decision the empty chair needs to inspect; the empty chair needs the decision and the chain of evidence the decision rested on.

The diagnostic technique that requires this primitive: empty-chair enumeration (Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}, Technique~1). The technique requires that, for each empty chair a control was meant to protect, the institution can demonstrate evidence that the implementation represents that chair. Demonstration requires evidence; evidence requires that the architecture preserved the chain from decision back to supporting material. Without evidence binding, demonstration reduces to assertion.

The property has two components. First, \emph{evidence preservation}: the architecture preserves the material that supported each decision in a form that survives institutional turnover, system migration, and ordinary records management. Second, \emph{traversal capability}: an examiner or other inspector can walk from a decision backward through the evidence that supported it, and forward from a piece of evidence to the decisions that relied on it. The two-direction traversal matters because empty-chair questions arrive in both forms: \emph{what evidence supported this decision} and \emph{what decisions depended on this evidence source}.

The empty chair this primitive most directly serves varies by which decision is being inspected. For an adverse action notice, the empty chair is the denied applicant, and evidence binding allows inspection of what the institution actually relied on (which may or may not match the post-hoc explanation produced for the notice). For a loan-pricing decision, the empty chair is the priced applicant, and evidence binding allows inspection of which underwriting features actually drove the rate. For a SAR filing decision, the empty chair is shared between the next victim of fraud (whose protection depended on the filing) and the customer who was reported (whose interest is in whether the filing was substantively warranted).

The property-level claim is distinct from logging: a system that logs decisions and logs evidence but does not bind them is not satisfying this primitive. The binding is what enables traversal. A pile of documents and a pile of decisions, both well-attested, are not evidence-bound unless the architecture preserves the relation between them.

A specific warning concerns explanation artifacts. Binding an explanation to a decision establishes that the explanation was associated with that decision. Whether it supports a further conclusion depends on the method, its evidentiary target, its configuration, and the inference claimed. A SHAP attribution may expose functional feature dependence under its specified formulation; a local surrogate may expose a local behavioral approximation. Neither association alone establishes a model's internal computational state, a human deliberative record, or causal influence on the decision. Evidence binding therefore establishes a traversable declared relationship between a decision and preserved evidence; it does not by itself prove causal influence or evidentiary sufficiency.

\subsection{Primitive 3: Three-dimensional confidence characterization}
\label{sec:primitives:confidence-characterization}

The property: the architecture characterizes the epistemic state of every assessment along three dimensions --- confidence (evidence supports the conclusion), concern (evidence supports an alternative conclusion), and indeterminacy (evidence is insufficient to determine either) --- rather than collapsing epistemic state to a single confidence score.

The diagnostic technique that requires this primitive: empty-chair enumeration applied to representation evidence (Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}, Technique~1). The technique asks whether the institution has evidence that the implementation represents each empty chair. The honest answer to this question, for many cases, is ``we do not know'': the institution's data does not establish that representation occurred, but neither does it establish that representation failed. The honest answer is indeterminacy, not confidence in a particular direction. A confidence-only architecture cannot express indeterminacy; it must report a confidence value, and the confidence value is then read as the institution's belief about the case, when the institution's actual epistemic state was ignorance.

The property has three components corresponding to the three dimensions. \emph{Confidence} measures evidence supporting the conclusion. \emph{Concern} measures evidence supporting alternatives. \emph{Indeterminacy} measures the insufficiency of evidence to determine either. The three are orthogonal: high confidence and high concern simultaneously indicates a contested case where evidence pulls both ways; low confidence and low concern with high indeterminacy indicates a case the institution does not have grounds to evaluate.

The empty chair this primitive serves is the affected party who depends on the institution's epistemic state being honestly characterized. A customer denied credit on a high-confidence assessment can challenge the assessment substantively. A customer denied credit on a high-indeterminacy assessment that was reported as moderate confidence has been deceived about the institution's actual state; the institution's reporting collapsed indeterminacy to a confidence value the institution did not actually have grounds for.

The frame proposes preserving support, counter-support, and insufficiency as distinct reported states. This representation avoids treating missing evidence as moderate confidence and makes contested cases visible. The paper does not prove that three dimensions are the only honest formalism, or that they are statistically appropriate for every model and decision. The requirement is functional: the chosen representation must not erase material counter-support or insufficiency.

\subsection{Primitive 4: Structural drift typology}
\label{sec:primitives:drift-typology}

The property: the architecture monitors and reports drift along distinct types --- data drift, concept drift, performance drift, and institutional drift --- rather than collapsing drift to a single performance metric.

The diagnostic technique that requires this primitive: produced-silence question pair (Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}, Technique~3). The technique asks what silences the architecture is producing and what it is inferring from those silences. Drift typology is structurally relevant because different drift types reveal different architectural patterns of silence-production. Performance drift may indicate model degradation; data drift may indicate population shifts the model was not trained for; concept drift may indicate that the relationships the model was trained on have changed; institutional drift may indicate divergence between de facto practice and de jure policy. Each is a different architectural fact about what the institution's deployment is doing, and each has different implications for empty-chair representation.

The property has four components corresponding to the four drift types. \emph{Data drift} tracks distributional changes in inputs. \emph{Concept drift} tracks changes in the relationship between inputs and the phenomena they predict. \emph{Performance drift} tracks changes in the model's outputs against ground truth or proxies. \emph{Institutional drift} tracks divergence between the institution's documented policy and its operational practice as captured in the attestation regime.

The empty chair varies by drift type. Data drift most directly affects the marginalized customer (whose population may have shifted in ways the model does not track). Concept drift affects the institution's future self (whose enforcement exposure depends on whether the model's predictions remain valid). Performance drift affects the next victim of fraud or the false-positive customer (whose exposure depends on the model's continued accuracy). Institutional drift affects the future compliance officer (who needs to understand how practice diverged from policy) and the examiner (whose review requires that documented policy match operational practice).

The proposed typology separates changes that call for different investigation. A performance-drift response (recalibration or retraining) differs from a data-drift response (population analysis or training-data review) and from an institutional-drift response (policy review or attestation-pattern audit). Detecting a drift type does not establish its cause; the typology directs inquiry rather than proving an explanation.

\subsection{What this section does not include}
\label{sec:primitives:exclusions}

Institutional self-knowledge is not listed as a fifth requirement. The present hypothesis is that durable knowledge may emerge from the four requirements operating together over time rather than from a separate technical primitive. Implementation work may disprove that hypothesis and show that knowledge continuity requires its own property-level specification.

We have also deliberately not included requirements that specify AI components themselves: model architecture choices, training methodology, ante-hoc interpretability techniques, or observation of internal states such as activations and intermediate representations~\parencite{mason2026epistemic,bricken2023monosemanticity}. Internal-state observations and generated text expose different evidentiary objects, each with method-specific limits. Selecting among them would commit this position paper to claims about model design that its governance argument does not establish. Those choices require separate research and validation.

\subsection{What the four primitives compound to}
\label{sec:primitives:compound}

Together, the four requirements can support the diagnostic techniques of Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics} and the empty chairs enumerated across Sections~\ref{sec:frame-as-method} and~\ref{sec:verification-regimes}. Tamper-evident temporal capture preserves declared decision inputs, context, and attestations; evidence binding makes declared relationships traversable; three-dimensional characterization preserves reported support, counter-support, and insufficiency; structural drift typology organizes investigation as conditions change.

This is the architectural direction the empty-chair frame proposes. It remains at the property level; multiple coherent implementations may exist, and the choice among them belongs to follow-on design and validation.

The requirements' contribution is a traceable connection from diagnostic question to architectural property. Their presence does not establish that an institution represents every affected party, and their absence does not prove that representation is impossible. They give architects and regulators concrete places to ask what evidence is preserved, what relationship is asserted, what uncertainty is reported, and what change is being investigated.

The honest uncertainty for this section, picked up in Section~\ref{sec:uncertainties}: we have argued for the four primitives from the structural commitments of the empty-chair frame and the diagnostic techniques the frame produces. We have not demonstrated that an implementation of the four primitives is operationally tractable for a community bank under cost and resource constraints. The frame's claim is structural; the implementation's tractability is empirical, and the empirical question is the work of Paper~2 and of subsequent deployment validation. The primitives' value in this paper is as the structural argument; their viability in practice is a question we do not resolve here.
