\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:implications}

The FS~AI~RMF gives financial institutions a substantial control vocabulary
\parencite{treasuryFSAIRMFRelease2026}. It does not, by itself, tell a community
bank how those controls cohere, tell an examiner what a completed artifact
warrants, or give either participant leverage to obtain missing evidence from a
vendor. Empty-chair representation supplies a coordination ontology for that
gap. It traces an affected interest through the control objective, required
evidentiary object, offered artifact, claimed inference and binding, and the
actor able to close, disclose, or contest the difference.

This is procedural neutrality rather than a view from nowhere. Affected parties
remain the normative center although they do not have power symmetric with a
bank, vendor, or regulator. Banks, examiners, regulators, and vendors receive
distinct roles because their obligations, authority, and capabilities differ.
The ontology makes those differences discussable without resolving them by
terminology. Evidentiary representation does not itself grant an affected party
access, participation, contest rights, or remedy.

The central constraint is that artifacts retain their method-specific warrants.
Decision-time records, feature attributions, local approximations,
internal-state observations, generated rationales, later reflective accounts,
human review records, and institutional approvals may all be useful. They
expose different evidentiary objects. An explanation reviewed by a human
establishes that the explanation was reviewed; a stronger conclusion depends on
what the method targets, what the reviewer could test, and how the evidence is
bound to the claim. Later reflection may discover genuine reasons, but should
be preserved as later reflection rather than substituted for a decision-time
record.

The evidentiary-substitution sequence names the neutral diagnostic structure;
silence-manufacture names instances in which unavailable contradictory evidence
helps the substitution persist, not a presumption of bad faith:
a required evidentiary object is unavailable, another artifact is offered, a
broader inference is claimed without an inspectable binding, contradictory
evidence is unavailable, and an affected party bears the gap. Produced absence
is likewise a hypothesis. Observed silence alone does not establish that a
design or institution caused it; comparison, counterfactual, provenance, user,
complaint, or externally sampled evidence must test the proposed mechanism.

The three diagnostics translate these distinctions into shared work.
Empty-chair enumeration identifies which interest depends on an artifact.
Pages missing from the ledger compares record coverage with a declared
attestation scope and operational population. The produced-silence question
asks what changed observability and who draws which inference from the resulting
record. Each begins as orientation. Under established authority it can structure
an evidence request; with sufficient evidence and applicable law or guidance it
may contribute to a finding. Otherwise, the result may properly remain a
disclosed limitation, procurement need, vendor design problem, or policy
question.

That division gives each participant a usable handoff. One bank can say which
proposition it is responsible for, which evidence it possesses, which available
process narrows the gap, which residual limitation remains, and which export it
needs from a vendor. That clarity does not give the bank bargaining power it
lacks.
An examiner can test a claimed inference without prescribing an unrequired
architecture. A regulator can aggregate recurring gaps and decide whether
guidance, coordinated expectations, or no further action is warranted. A vendor
can specify which provenance, exportability, temporal, uncertainty, and
monitoring capabilities its system supplies. Cross-vendor bindings, standard
exports, and market leverage require coordinated infrastructure that no single
institution can produce. An affected party can identify the
interest and proposition at stake, supply evidence, and invoke any applicable
complaint, appeal, correction, or review process. Where no such pathway exists,
the ontology identifies the absence and who bears it; it does not cure the
absence by calling the interest represented.

Five provisional evidentiary capabilities suggest where technical and
contractual work might help: revision-aware temporal capture; evidence binding
and exportability; non-collapsing uncertainty representation; technical-change
monitoring; and institutional-conformance monitoring. They are design
hypotheses, not mandates. Their presence does not prove a governance claim,
their absence does not prove a violation, and their cost and viability for
community banks remain unvalidated.

For a participant reading or contesting an AI governance record, the frame
reduces to five questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item What evidentiary object does this artifact expose?
  \item What proposition is the institution asking the recipient to infer?
  \item What inspectable binding warrants that inference?
  \item Whose interest bears the gap, and under what authority may it be tested?
  \item Which actor can close or disclose the gap, by what remedy?
\end{enumerate}

Those questions do not, by themselves, decide every governance dispute or create authority that
law and policy have not supplied. They make the location and ownership of
disagreement legible. A useful governance architecture should let an affected
party's interest appear not only in policy language, but in evidence capable of
bearing the conclusion placed upon it.
