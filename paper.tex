\begin{abstract}
The Financial Services AI Risk Management Framework (FS~AI~RMF) specifies 230
control objectives without resolving how a community bank should interpret
commensurate compliance as a coherent system. This paper proposes
\emph{empty-chair representation} as an organizing lens: ask which party is
absent when an AI-supported decision is made, which interest the control is
meant to represent, and what evidence would make that representation
inspectable. Worked examples bind the lens to specific FS~AI~RMF control
objectives; they demonstrate the method rather than claim a coding of the full
framework.

The central verification problem is an artifact-to-inference gap. A decision
record, feature attribution, local surrogate, generated rationale, human review
record, and approval signature expose different evidentiary objects. Governance
fails when an artifact is treated as establishing a different proposition
without an inspectable warrant. We call the recurring pattern
\emph{silence-manufacture} and develop three regulator-usable diagnostics:
empty-chair enumeration, pages missing from the ledger, and the produced-silence
question pair. The diagnostics generate four provisional architectural
requirements: tamper-evident temporal capture, evidence binding,
multidimensional characterization of support and insufficiency, and structural
drift typology. These requirements are property-level design hypotheses, not a
validated implementation. The contribution is a bounded vocabulary for asking
what an AI governance artifact establishes, what inference is claimed from it,
and whose interests bear the gap.
\end{abstract}
